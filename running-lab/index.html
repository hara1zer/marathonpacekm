A transparent process

How the Lab works

A personal record can be useful without being a controlled experiment. What matters is being clear about what is known and what is still a question.

  1. Start with a practical question. What decision would a runner make differently?
  2. Gather the original observations. Use recorded laps, training totals and personal notes; label recollections as recollections.
  3. Analyse in context. Check the arithmetic and relevant research without treating association as a cause.
  4. Show the boundary. Separate recorded observations, calculated scenarios, interpretations and unresolved gaps.
  5. Make the next step useful. Offer a practical takeaway, a record to keep or an existing tool for the decision.

From investigation to action

Tools for your own running questions

The studies explain why a question matters. These tools let you explore your own inputs; their outputs do not establish what caused a past race result.

  • Marathon time predictor

    Test the conversion assumptions discussed in the Sydney review. A projected time is a scenario, not proof of readiness.

  • Pace calculator

    Build your own elapsed checkpoints. Keep the planned schedule separate from the watch-lap observations.

  • Fueling calculator

    Count the actual servings in a chosen schedule and compare its full-run average with a target.

  • Race conditions pace adjuster

    Explore an explicitly hypothetical conditions adjustment. The Lab does not invent weather data for past races.

  • Monthly training planner

    Include all four recent weeks, including interruptions, when organising a proposed training block.

Personal evidence, with its limits visible

The founding collection is my own running: two Run Melbourne half marathons, a Sydney Marathon build and race, personal gel trials and the rebuild I intend to make. A proposed plan is not a completed experiment; a watch split is not an official course checkpoint.

The original articles retain their source notes and publication dates. The Sydney and Melbourne articles include downloadable transcribed data. Missing observations are left missing. No product comparison here is controlled, and no calculator supplies medical clearance or a guarantee of race performance.
