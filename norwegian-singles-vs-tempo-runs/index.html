Norwegian Singles vs tempo runs: compare structure before pace

“Tempo” can mean several different workouts. Compare uninterrupted effort, work minutes and recovery before deciding whether two session labels are interchangeable.

First write down what tempo means in your plan

A coach or club may use tempo for steady running, continuous threshold work or a progression. Without a duration and intended effort, the label does not establish intensity. Describe the actual session before comparing it with controlled intervals.

The single describes the quality-day arrangement

On this site a single is one controlled threshold session on a quality day. Its repetitions and breaks can vary. The overview explains why that is not a standardized recreational protocol.

The interval versions allow checks and breaks. That does not establish an identical physiological stimulus or prove a lower recovery cost for every runner.

The breaks are not permission to race the repetitions

If you run the interval version much faster because a break is coming, you have changed both structure and intensity. To compare the effect of structure, start from the intended effort and record actual pace, recoveries and later repetitions. The calculator supplies an estimate, not a laboratory boundary.

Do not rank fatigue from the label alone

A short steady tempo and a long interval session are not comparable solely because both contain the word threshold. Count total work and inspect the next day’s response. Terrain, conditions and current preparation can matter more than whether the work was continuous.

A continuous segment answers a continuity question

If the question is whether you can hold the intended effort without recovery breaks, a continuous segment makes that observation clearer. Its duration still needs to fit your training. Record whether the effort stayed stable rather than judging success by the final fast kilometre.

Broken work offers repeated decision points

Use the gaps to decide whether pace remains appropriate, adjust the next repetition or finish the work early. Those decisions are useful when learning to regulate effort. They are less useful when every gap is used to recover from a repetition run too hard.

Neither label proves marathon readiness

A 24-minute session does not reproduce the duration, fueling or late fatigue of a marathon. Keep the comparison separate from choosing marathon pace. See tempo, threshold and marathon pace and the marathon adaptation.

Compare one variable at a time

  • Changing structure, speed and duration together.
  • Counting recovery time as work time.
  • Calling every moderately hard run threshold.
  • Inferring recovery cost from a workout name.

Structure FAQ

Is a tempo run always continuous threshold running?

No. Tempo is used for different durations and efforts. Check what the actual plan means before comparing sessions.

Are broken repetitions automatically easier to recover from?

No. Total work, intensity, conditions and your response matter. The breaks alone do not establish the recovery cost.
