Even splits, negative splits and a strong finish

Pacing decisions

Choose a strategy by its total time, the course and your preparation. A controlled opening can be sensible without committing to an exact negative split.

Three different ideas

  • Even halves: each half takes the same time. Even kilometre pace is a more detailed version of the same planning idea.
  • Negative split: the second half takes less time than the first.
  • Controlled effort: pace can vary with terrain and conditions. This does not guarantee either an even or negative split.

Starting two minutes behind even pace at halfway requires a four-minute difference between halves to retain four hours. A slower opening is not free: check whether the later requirement fits your preparation.

Choose a plan you can explain

On a flat course in stable conditions, even pace is a useful reference. A slightly slower opening may help you settle in crowds. On a hilly or exposed course, forcing identical splits may create unwanted effort changes.

A negative split is an option, not a requirement for beginners or proof of good execution. Terrain can make the second half slower even in a well-managed race. For more detail, read negative-split planning.

A strong finish can matter more than the label

My 2025 and 2026 Run Melbourne halves show a clear contrast: I struggled late in 2025 and finished strongly in 2026. The final two full recorded kilometres in 2026 were 4:53 and 4:51. Those laps support the story of a strong finish, but they do not establish exact race-half times.

Use checkpoints to revise, not defend, the target

Compare the opening with your planned pace, then reassess effort and conditions at later checkpoints. If maintaining the target becomes unrealistic, calculate what it would require over the remaining distance and decide whether to revise it.

At 30 km, difficulty can have several causes. It does not prove that the opening was too fast. Avoid chasing time with a sudden surge or assuming that a fixed small adjustment will prevent a later fade.

Build the reference

Use the pace calculator for precise checkpoints and the pace-band generator for even, controlled-start or negative-split shapes. The bands preserve the requested total; they do not validate that total for you.

Questions about split strategy

Is a negative split always better?

No. Preparation, the course and conditions determine whether it is sensible. A strong finish is not the same as an exact negative split.

How much slower should I start?

There is no universal allowance. Include any slower opening in the total arithmetic and check the later pace requirement.

Can I abandon the planned split?

Yes. Health, effort and actual conditions take priority over a printed target.
