Who 5:00:00 pace suits

  • First-time marathoners aiming to finish comfortably, or runners returning after a break.
  • Runners targeting a 5:00:00 finish who can hold steady effort without big surges.
  • People who like a simple plan: controlled start, settle into rhythm, protect pace after 30 km.
  • Those willing to fuel early and consistently (late fatigue can have several contributors).
  • Anyone choosing run/walk — it can be very effective at this pace if planned.

Common mistakes at 5:00:00 pace

  • Starting too fast: the “free speed” at 0–10 km isn’t free — it costs you after 30 km.
  • Too many unscheduled walk breaks: if you walk, plan it (short + consistent beats random).
  • Over-correcting GPS: don’t surge for every pace blip; use lap pace + effort.
  • Skipping fuel early: waiting until you feel bad is too late. Start within 20–30 minutes.
  • Letting aid stations break rhythm: slow slightly, drink, then return to cadence quickly.

Evaluate a run/walk plan

Running and walking speeds both contribute to the average. Choose a pattern you have practised, then compare its full-cycle distance and elapsed time with the target. The worked example below shows the mixed-speed arithmetic. The example is not a treatment for pain, cramps or injury.

Pacing Plan (simple and realistic)

  • 0–5 km: start easier than you think. The goal is to feel relaxed.
  • 5–30 km: settle into steady effort around 7:07/km (or your planned run/walk).
  • 30–40 km: keep it boring: cadence, posture, sip fluids, keep the plan.
  • 40–42.2 km: if you’re still “together”, shorten focus to the next landmark and finish strong.

Plan fuel for 5:00 by grams and elapsed time

Choose an hourly intake you have practised, check each product's carbohydrate content and include planned drinks. A finish-time goal alone does not determine how many gels you need.

For example, six 25 g gels contain 150 g. Across 5:00:00 that averages only 30.0 g/h before drinks. If you chose 60 g/h as a practiced target, the race total would be about 300 g. These are arithmetic comparisons, not personalised intake recommendations.

The fueling calculator shows the actual average supplied by the selected schedule as well as your target. Account for product tolerance, station availability and what you actually consume. Distance-running nutrition guidance supports personalising and rehearsing the plan.

Check whether 5:00 fits your current training

A goal pace is arithmetic, not an individual training prescription. Compare a recent race with your sustained weekly running, long-run recovery and ability to keep marathon-specific work controlled. There is no universal weekly distance or fixed easy-pace offset that proves this target is right for you.

Keep easy running at an effort that lets you recover. A faster interval session can demonstrate speed without demonstrating the ability to hold 7:06.6/km for a marathon. My Sydney preparation is an example of that distinction.

5:00 Marathon Pacing FAQ

What pace per km is a 5:00 marathon?

A 5:00:00 marathon requires about 7:07 per km on average (42.195 km in 5 hours).

What are the key split times for a 5:00 marathon?

Most runners track 5k, 10k, half marathon, 30k, 40k, and the finish. Use the calculator to generate exact splits.

Should I run even splits for a 5:00 marathon?

Steady pacing usually wins. Keep the first half controlled at goal pace and aim to avoid early surges.

Five hours includes the walks and the stops

The required average is about 7:06.6/km across the full race. If you use a repeated run/walk cycle, the running portions need to be faster than that average. For a 4-minute run/1-minute walk cycle, a five-minute cycle must cover about 0.703 km. With the walk at an illustrative 10:00/km, the running portion would need about 6:37.8/km.

This calculation assumes the cycle repeats without extra stationary time. It is a planning example, not a prescription to use 4:1 or walk at a particular speed. Rehearse a sustainable combination and allow for station queues, transitions and the course.

Check the logistics of spending five hours on course

Review the organiser's course cutoff rules, station locations and available products for your actual event. A five-hour finish goal does not establish that every intermediate cutoff will be met, especially if the timing rule differs from your chip start.

Plan fuel by elapsed time and count the grams from all products. A longer finish time usually means more total carrying or resupply needs for the same chosen hourly intake. It does not establish that a particular number of gels will suit everyone. Make sure the final hour of your plan is as deliberate as the first.

Include a stop without confusing it with walking

If you stand still for one minute in total, keeping 5:00:00 would require covering the distance in 4:59:00, averaging 7:05.2/km while moving. This example assumes no distance is covered during the stop. Walking is different because it still contributes distance.

Make a plan using a pace band for this exact target. For a controlled start or negative split, the tool recalculates the later pace needed to preserve the finish time. Neither option guarantees that the required effort is sustainable.
