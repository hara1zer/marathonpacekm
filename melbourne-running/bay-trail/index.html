Choose your foreshore section first

The eastern Bay Trail network extends from the Port Melbourne area near the West Gate Bridge towards Seaford and Austin Road, with wetlands in the southern sections. This guide uses that extent without assigning a single precise length to the whole network.

The community route guide describes sealed sections north of Mordialloc and gravel sections farther south. Select the section and surface that suit the intended run rather than treating a St Kilda foreshore workout and a southern wetland run as the same route. A coastal setting does not establish identical terrain or lighting throughout.

Events and weekend sharing

Port Phillip’s current event notifications document organised running on the St Kilda–Elwood foreshore. Read the notices before choosing a faster weekend session: an event can change access, the running line and how much space is available.

Bayside’s 2013 bicycle strategy records weekend popularity and conflict between path users. That older document explains why sharing space matters, but it is not a current crowd measurement. Give walkers, runners and cyclists room and slow where an overtaking manoeuvre is constrained.

The event notices are more useful for a particular visit than a generic claim that the coast is always crowded at a set hour. Arrange a marathon-pace block on a section and date where the current conditions allow it; keep a slower or shorter alternative available if they do not.

Wind and the return leg

An out-and-back on the foreshore gives you a practical way to observe changing effort in opposite directions. If the outward leg benefits from the wind, leave time and energy for the return rather than budgeting both legs at the same pace. Use the conditions you actually encounter, not an assumed prevailing wind direction.

For race preparation, that can make the route useful for practising coastal effort decisions. It is less useful as a narrow pace test when the wind materially changes the workload. Record which direction felt harder so the session’s average pace remains interpretable.

A specific refill and toilet point

Port Phillip lists a drinking fountain and public and accessible toilets at Marina Reserve. Use that named point when planning a St Kilda-area run. Its facilities do not establish refill intervals for the rest of the Bay Trail.

Carry supplies for the section between your chosen facilities. If your session includes a stop or a detour to the reserve, allow for it explicitly. Event drink stations belong to an organised event and should not form the facilities plan for an ordinary training run.

Transport and visibility

Bayside’s Sandringham foreshore planning documents identify rail access in the middle of the network. Sandringham can be a useful access area when the chosen section suits your distance plan. Check current train services and the connection from the station to the actual running start.

Choose daylight for a section you do not know, and read current signs where the surface or route changes. This guide does not assign a whole-network after-dark lighting schedule. If visibility or access makes the planned return uncertain, shorten the section rather than relying on the trail’s total geographical extent.

When the Bay Trail fits

Use it for a planned coastal out-and-back, with the start, turnaround, supplies and return transport decided together. For a steady session that needs less influence from changing wind, compare Albert Park Lake. For a western measured track session, Newport Park has separate lane-use rules and group-hire arrangements.

Frequently asked questions

Does this guide give one exact Bay Trail length?

No. It describes the Port Melbourne-to-Seaford network and helps plan a selected section without assigning a conflicting precise total.

Where is a named water and toilet point?

Port Phillip lists a drinking fountain and public and accessible toilets at Marina Reserve. That does not establish evenly spaced facilities along the network.

What should I check before a St Kilda–Elwood run?

Read Port Phillip's current running-event and closure notices for the chosen date and section.

How should I plan the return leg in wind?

Use the actual conditions. If the outward leg benefits from the wind, allow time and effort for a harder return rather than assuming the same pace in both directions.
