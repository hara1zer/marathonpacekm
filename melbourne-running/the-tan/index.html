The route and what changes the effort

Run the Tan’s official lap starts at the Pillars of Wisdom and covers 3.827 km clockwise around the Royal Botanic Gardens and Kings Domain. Head along Alexandra Avenue, turn up Anderson Street, then follow the perimeter towards Birdwood Avenue and back to Alexandra Avenue. Keep the gardens on your right for this published circuit.

The main surface is fine gravel, with a sealed section on Anderson Street. The City of Melbourne describes the hill as rising about 30 m over 300 m. That figure describes the climb, rather than the elevation gain of the whole circuit. Ease your pace uphill and judge the effort before trying to recover seconds on the descent.

Plan 10 km, 21.1 km or 30 km

The table uses 3.827 km per official lap. The added distances are arithmetic remainders, not surveyed extensions or marked finish points. After your full laps, continue on the circuit for the remaining distance you have planned. If you finish part-way around, allow separately for getting back to your starting point.

The 21.1 km option is a rounded training target, rather than a certified half-marathon course.

GPS readings can differ from the published lap distance. Use the arithmetic to plan, then your watch or markers to manage a partial lap. Leave room for other users at the finish.

What the Anderson Street climb changes

Each clockwise lap includes the Anderson Street climb. Use effort on that section rather than trying to hold the same flat-ground pace throughout. For a longer run, the repeated climb becomes part of the session's workload as well as its distance.

Choose the Tan when that rolling circuit fits the planned run. If the session needs a narrow pace range on flatter ground, compare Albert Park Lake or a measured track instead.

Crowding, interruptions and practical planning

The council’s run-club listing schedules Saturday sessions from 8–10 am. Run the Tan also documents established clubs and annual events here. That supports its place in a Melbourne training shortlist and gives you a concrete period to consider when arranging a faster session.

Give groups and walkers room, and slow down before passing where visibility is limited. Follow current signs at path junctions and any works diversions. If an interruption breaks a tempo block, record it and continue by effort rather than accelerating abruptly to make the watch average match a target.

Water, toilets, lighting and transport

The organiser lists nearby toilets along Alexandra Avenue, at the Sidney Myer Music Bowl and inside the gardens. The council’s Alexandra Gardens site guide documents nearby drinking fountains and public toilets around Boathouse Drive. Those amenities are in the wider precinct. Carry water for a long run, and use current signs to find a working refill and an open toilet.

Run the Tan reports lighting between 5 am and midnight. Choose a start time that gives you enough visibility to read the gravel surface and see other users. Lighting alone cannot establish after-dark safety. For a dark session, arrange company where useful, carry a light if needed and reassess conditions when you arrive.

The council identifies Flinders Street Station and St Kilda Road tram stops as access points for nearby Alexandra Gardens. Plan a walking connection to your chosen Tan start, or include that distance explicitly in your run. A transport connection adds distance beyond the 3.827 km circuit, so keep it separate from the lap totals.

Frequently asked questions

How long is one lap of The Tan?

Run the Tan publishes an official lap distance of 3.827 km. Its event course starts and finishes at the Pillars of Wisdom and runs clockwise, keeping the gardens on the right.

How many Tan laps make 30 km?

Seven official laps total 26.789 km, leaving a calculated 3.211 km for a 30 km session. Eight laps total 30.616 km and return you to your start. GPS readings can differ from the published lap arithmetic.

Is The Tan suitable for marathon-pace training?

It can suit effort-based blocks, but the Anderson Street climb and shared path make constant pace less useful. Ease the pace on the climb, give other users room and compare your effort across repeat laps.

Is The Tan lit after dark?

Run the Tan reports lighting from 5 am to midnight. Lighting does not establish personal safety or guarantee that nearby toilets or garden gates are open. Check conditions and take a light if visibility requires it.
