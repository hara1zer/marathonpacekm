Why 6:00/km and 4:15 are not interchangeable

The 4:15 average is 6:02.6/km. Running exactly 6:00/km over the full course takes about 4:13:10. That leaves around 1:50 compared with 4:15, before allowing for any stops. An illustrative 90-second stationary stop would bring that particular calculation to about 4:14:40.

This is useful for comparing a continuous plan with one that includes stops, not a recommendation to run faster so you can stop. Walking still covers distance; model a run/walk strategy separately. Your ability to maintain the moving pace needs to come from training.

A deliberate goal between four hours and four-and-a-half

Choosing 4:15 can be a considered race target in its own right. It need not be a failed sub-four attempt. Use the 2:07:30 halfway checkpoint and the later splits to decide what a sustainable plan looks like for your recent training and expected conditions.

If you are comparing goals, 4:00 marathon pace averages about 5:41.3/km and 4:30 marathon pace about 6:23.9/km. Those twenty-odd seconds per kilometre on either side are meaningful over several hours. Test the effort and recovery in your existing training rather than choosing a number solely because it sits between two familiar milestones.

Understand a planned run/walk cycle

Consider a ten-minute cycle: eight minutes running at 5:50/km and two walking at 10:00/km. Compute distance in each phase, then compare the cycle average with the goal.

Eight running minutes cover 1.371 km; two walking minutes cover 0.200 km. The cycle covers 1.571 km in ten minutes, averaging 6:21.8/km, compared with 6:02.6/km for 4:15. A complete-cycle average extrapolates to 4:28:31, but the race ends partway through the next cycle: 26 complete cycles cover 40.857 km, leaving 1.338 km running at 5:50/km. The actual modeled finish is 4:27:48, before any other pauses. The final partial phase matters. This is an illustrative cycle, not a prescribed strategy.
