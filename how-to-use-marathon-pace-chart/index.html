How to use a marathon pace chart

Pacing decisions

A pace chart converts a finish target into cumulative checkpoints. Use it to understand the plan and the consequences of changing it, while keeping effort and conditions in view.

By Davin Pinto · Updated 1 October 2026 · Editorial policy · Corrections

Choose a supported target

Start with representative recent races, the training you normally sustain and the course. A predictor gives a scenario, not a safe or likely time. Choose the goal before using the pace calculator to create precise checkpoints.

Read an even four-hour example

On a small screen, swipe the table to see all columns.

Cumulative times use the full-precision pace before rounding. The quick label 5:41/km is rounded and does not reconstruct exactly four hours. A sub-four goal must be below four hours; set that exact target in the calculator.

Distinguish segment and cumulative time

The value beside 30 km is the total to that point. To find a segment, subtract two cumulative values. In this example, 30–40 km takes 3:47:31 minus 2:50:38 = 56:53. Do not mistake a ten-kilometre segment for a kilometre pace.

Use the difference as information

If crowding or a climb puts you behind, first understand the cause. Returning to the original effort does not automatically recover lost time. For a four-hour target, reaching 30 km in 2:55 requires about 5:20/km for the remaining 12.195 km. Review whether retaining the target makes sense.

Allow for the actual course

Even pace is a useful reference on a stable course, but hills, wind and heat can change sustainable effort. Read the adjustment guides and follow event advice. A chart cannot determine whether continuing through symptoms is safe.

Make a readable reference

Use the pace-band generator for even, controlled-start or negative-split plans. Confirm units and the finish time. A slower opening keeps the same requested total only by changing later checkpoints. Fuel reminders belong in the separate fuel schedule.

Practise interpretation in ordinary training

Use a familiar watch screen and notice the difference between instant pace, lap pace and cumulative time. GPS lap distance can be inaccurate around obstructions. Check the GPS guide rather than accelerating to correct a single implausible reading.

Questions about charts

Must I hit every split exactly?

No. The chart is a reference. Terrain, crowds, conditions and your response can justify changes.

Does choosing a slower opening preserve the finish automatically?

Only in the calculation. The later pace required may not be sustainable in the race.

Which units should I choose?

Use kilometres or miles according to the course and the reference you find easiest to read. Neither unit is more accurate.
