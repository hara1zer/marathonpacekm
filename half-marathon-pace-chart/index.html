Half-marathon pace chart: kilometre and mile splits

By Davin Pinto · Published 4 November 2026 · Updated 10 November 2026

Choose a finish time to find your average pace and cumulative checkpoints for 21.0975 km. The full chart covers 1:15 to 3:30; the calculator accepts a custom target or a pace outside those rows.

For example, a 2:00:00 half marathon averages 5:41.3/km or 9:09.2/mile. At even speed, the 5 km checkpoint is 28:26 and the 20 km checkpoint is 1:53:45. You still have 1.0975 km to run after 20 km.

How do I use these checkpoints during the race?

Choose a target supported by your recent running before choosing a row. A fast 5 km result and an average-pace calculation answer different questions. If you are deciding how to distribute effort, use the half-marathon pacing strategy guide; it compares an even schedule with a slower opening and includes recorded Run Melbourne examples.

Compare the schedule with elapsed time at official course markers. A watch kilometre beep may not occur at the sign, and a large clock may show time from the starting gun rather than from your own start. Auto-pause can hide time that still counts towards the finish.

Hills, wind, crowds and drink stops can change individual segments. A chart does not require you to recover every missed second immediately. Use your effort and the remaining course when deciding whether the original schedule still fits. The GPS pacing guide explains why watch distance can disagree with the measured route.

Take a readable reference with you using the half-marathon pace-band generator. It supports kilometre or mile checkpoints, a wrist band and a phone card. Use the half-marathon taper worksheet for the separate task of organising the last training days.

How are half-marathon pace and splits calculated?

Average pace in seconds per kilometre is finish time in seconds divided by 21.0975. Multiply that unrounded pace by each checkpoint distance. To express the same speed in min/mile, multiply seconds per kilometre by 1.609344.

At two hours, 7,200 ÷ 21.0975 = approximately 341.273 seconds/km. Multiplying the rounded label 5:41/km back over the whole course gives about 1:59:54, rather than exactly two hours. The checkpoint calculations retain the original target.

The familiar label “21.1 km” is rounded. The final marker in the calculator uses 21.0975 km. For context on what a half-marathon result might mean for a later full marathon, read half-to-full marathon prediction; its scenarios do not replace the race-day schedule here.

Does a two-hour half require 5:41/km on my watch?

5:41 is a rounded display of the average. Watch distance, rounding and stops can all matter. Cumulative elapsed checkpoints are a better arithmetic reference than multiplying a displayed pace.

Can I use the chart for a run-walk half marathon?

The average-speed row still describes the target, but your running intervals would need to account for walking and stops. This page does not calculate a half-marathon run-walk plan. The site's run-walk calculator currently models the full marathon distance.
