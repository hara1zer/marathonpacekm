Norwegian Singles workouts: count the work, recovery and total time

Compare sessions by actual dose. These illustrations explain the arithmetic and the decisions to record; they do not assign workouts from a goal race time.

The label does not specify the whole workout

A useful log includes repetition duration, total work, recovery, surface, conditions and how you felt afterward. A “single” quality session can still be too demanding if you run it as a series of races. See the terminology guide for the limits of the label.

Use a starting estimate and check the repetitions

The calculator’s stated offsets are heuristics. They do not measure threshold. Compare early and late repetitions without sprinting to preserve the average; reduce pace, reduce the work or stop when control has gone. Record the response of your next easy run too.

Even these shorter examples add a demanding session to the week. Someone new to running can keep building easy-running consistency instead. The ten-minute warm-up and cooldown are arithmetic assumptions here, not universal requirements.

Shorter repetitions: avoid changing duration and speed together

Six repetitions of four minutes give 24 minutes of work. With five one-minute recoveries and ten minutes each to warm up and cool down, the session takes 49 minutes. If each repetition becomes five minutes, work rises to 30 minutes, a 25% increase before any pace change. A short repetition does not justify turning it into 5K race effort.

Longer repetitions: the same minutes can feel different

Four six-minute repetitions and three eight-minute repetitions both provide 24 work minutes. With one-minute recoveries between reps, the first includes three recovery minutes and the second two. The longer uninterrupted effort and fewer breaks change the session even though the work total matches.

Keep race-specific work as a separate category

Four eight-minute controlled repetitions supply 32 minutes of faster work; that is not a substitute for a long run or a test of several hours of fueling. Do not relabel the pace “marathon” solely because it appears in a marathon plan. My Sydney workout review shows that problem in my own log.

Treadmill versions: record the units and setup

At 12 km/h the belt setting corresponds to 5:00/km; at 11.5 km/h it is about 5:13/km. These conversions are exact relationships, not an outdoor-equivalence guarantee. Record belt speed, incline, room conditions and cooling. Do not assume one incline setting makes all treadmills equivalent to road running.

Use a repeatable comparison

Repeat a tolerated structure under reasonably similar conditions before changing it. Record whether later repetitions stayed controlled and whether normal training resumed. Increase work, change recoveries or change pace as separate decisions. There is no mandatory weekly ladder in these examples.

Log the whole session

  • Comparing 24 work minutes with 24 total minutes.
  • Forgetting that four repetitions have three between-rep recoveries.
  • Giving every repetition a faster finish to make the average look better.
  • Increasing the workout while also increasing the long run.

Workout arithmetic FAQ

Do 4 × 6 minutes and 3 × 8 minutes have the same dose?

They both have 24 work minutes, but repetition length and number of recoveries differ. Equal work time does not make their demands identical.

Are these scientifically validated recreational sessions?

No. They are comparisons and scheduling illustrations by this site. The research links explain the broader training context.
