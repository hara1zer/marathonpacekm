Melbourne Marathon 2026: the revised course, stations and pacing decisions

The 2026 edition to plan for

The organiser lists Sunday 11 October 2026, 6:15am, starting on Batman Avenue and finishing at the MCG. Check the 2026 event page before race day.

The 2026 course announcement describes a reversed direction toward Beach Road, removal of the late Birdwood Avenue / Domain Road climb and fewer turns. These changes do not establish a personal time improvement; compare the new route with your own preparation.

Use the forecast for exposure, not a monthly maximum

The beachfront is where a change of direction can change the wind you feel. A cool start does not establish the temperature later in your race, and a historical monthly maximum is not a race-hour forecast. Check temperature, wind direction and gusts for your expected finish window. The conditions tool offers scenarios rather than a course-specific guarantee.

Three decisions to settle before the start

  1. Use the revised 2026 map, not a previous Melbourne route.
  2. Choose an effort response for the exposed outbound and return segments rather than a fixed headwind pace penalty.
  3. Match your carried fuel to the station products and distances below; do not assume gels are available at every Maurten station.

The late-course change is not free pace

Removing a previous climb changes the route, but it does not remove thirty-plus kilometres of accumulated fatigue. Do not bank extra time early on the assumption that the redesigned finish will be easy. Compare effort at 33 km with what you rehearsed; use a later target if the original pace requires an unsustainable push.

Wind pacing and heart rate and perceived effort explain the general checks.

A four-hour example at the gel stations

At four-hour even pace, 25 km is 2:22:12 and 33 km is 3:07:42. The 8 km gap is about 45:30. A plan that needs fuel more often cannot depend only on those two gel stations. Carry the servings your schedule requires and verify the exact variant provided.

For example, eight carried 25 g gels plus 40 g from verified drinks supply 240 g over four hours, or 60 g/h. That is a hypothetical menu using an already practiced intake; it is not a promise about the carbohydrate in Melbourne’s cups. Use the fueling calculator and complete schedules.

Set elapsed time and use measured markers

Keep total elapsed time visible so station pauses stay in the plan. Compare a measured kilometre marker with the relevant cumulative checkpoint instead of trying to correct every noisy GPS pace reading. GPS and tangent guidance covers that distinction.

The pace-band generator prints checkpoints. Its output does not include gel reminders; save the separate fuel schedule as well.

Melbourne 2026 FAQ

Can I reuse last year’s course plan?

Check it against the revised 2026 map first. The organiser describes changes to direction, turns and the late climb.

Are gels available at every drink station?

No. The published event-page table lists Maurten gels at 25 and 33 km. Check for late changes and carry what your rehearsed schedule needs.

Are the four-hour station times official split times?

No. They are elapsed-time calculations using 42.195 km and even pace. They include any pauses and do not predict actual arrival.
