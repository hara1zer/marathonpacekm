Pacing decisions

How to use a marathon pace band

A pace band puts planned cumulative times beside course distances. It can make a checkpoint easier to read, but it cannot decide whether the target remains appropriate.

By Davin Pinto · Updated 4 November 2026

On this page

  • Read cumulative time correctly
  • Choose the target before the design
  • Choose the pacing shape
  • Use it at meaningful checkpoints
  • Prepare the reference
  • Print-and-run record
  • Keep fuel reminders separate
  • Generate your band

Read cumulative time correctly

The number beside 10 km is the planned total at 10 km, not the time for the previous five kilometres. For an even four-hour marathon, 5 km is 28:26 and 10 km is 56:53. The displayed 5–10 km segment is therefore 28:27 because the cumulative values are rounded.

Choose the target before the design

Use a goal supported by representative races, normal training and current conditions. The goal guide explains the decision. If the target changes, regenerate the band instead of trying to mentally reinterpret every row.

Choose the pacing shape

The pace-band generator supports even, controlled-start and negative-split plans. Each retains your requested finish time. A slower opening therefore requires faster later running. The methods page explains the curves; they are planning choices, not validated optimal strategies.

Use it at meaningful checkpoints

  • Compare a reliable course marker with the cumulative target.
  • Consider terrain, weather and effort before reacting to a difference.
  • Avoid a surge to recover time lost in a crowd or at a station.
  • Revise the goal if maintaining it would require an inappropriate effort.

At 30 km in 2:55, a four-hour finish requires about 5:20/km for the remaining 12.195 km. A band makes the difference visible; it does not make that later pace sustainable.

How to print, size and attach your pace band

  1. Check the plan first. Confirm marathon or half marathon, kilometre or mile markers, finish time and pacing strategy. Select the wrist-band layout rather than the phone card.
  2. Match the paper. Select A4 or Letter in the generator and in the print dialog, with landscape orientation. Use actual size or 100% scale rather than “fit to page”. Save a PDF first if you want to inspect the layout.
  3. Measure before cutting. The printed calibration line should measure 50 mm. If it does not, correct scaling and print again. The three copies on the sheet are alternatives; each complete strip should stay on one page.
  4. Try the fit. Start with ordinary printer paper and the selected wrist size. Cut one strip, keep the labelled overlap and check that it closes comfortably. Do not tighten it against the skin to compensate for choosing a smaller size.
  5. Choose and rehearse the fastening. Clear tape over both sides is one practical option to try. Trim edges carefully and join at the overlap. Check comfort, readability and whether your chosen cover or fastening survives your own rehearsal.

Paper, ink, tape and conditions vary. These are preparation instructions, not a claim that a particular material is waterproof or that I have completed a field comparison. Keep a backup reference where practical.

For a two-hour half marathon, start with the half-marathon chart, then open the same target in the band generator.

A print-and-run record for your own rehearsal

Record the material and settings before trying the band. After the run, note what you could actually read, whether it moved, and how the fastening and paper looked. A single rehearsal describes that band in those conditions; it cannot establish a universal durability ranking.

  • Printer / paper / cover / fastening: __________________
  • Paper setting / scale / measured calibration: __________________
  • Wrist setting / fit before the run: __________________
  • Duration / conditions / sweat or rain exposure: __________________
  • Readability while moving / fit after the run: __________________
  • Change to make before race day: __________________

Download the blank test record (CSV). Keep photos and observations together if you want to compare a later version.

Keep fuel reminders separate

The band contains pacing checkpoints. It does not include a gel schedule. Use the fueling calculator for separate reminders and the fueling guide to check totals and station access.

Generate your band

Open the custom generator or start from a goal page such as four-hour pace. Read GPS and course-line guidance if watch distance and course markers disagree.
