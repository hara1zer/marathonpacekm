Pacing decisions

How to use a marathon pace band

A pace band puts planned cumulative times beside course distances. It can make a checkpoint easier to read, but it cannot decide whether the target remains appropriate.

By Davin Pinto · Updated 1 October 2026 · Editorial policy · Corrections

On this page

  • Read cumulative time correctly
  • Choose the target before the design
  • Choose the pacing shape
  • Use it at meaningful checkpoints
  • Prepare the reference
  • Keep fuel reminders separate
  • Generate your band

Read cumulative time correctly

The number beside 10 km is the planned total at 10 km, not the time for the previous five kilometres. For an even four-hour marathon, 5 km is 28:26 and 10 km is 56:53. The displayed 5–10 km segment is therefore 28:27 because the cumulative values are rounded.

Choose the target before the design

Use a goal supported by representative races, normal training and current conditions. The goal guide explains the decision. If the target changes, regenerate the band instead of trying to mentally reinterpret every row.

Choose the pacing shape

The pace-band generator supports even, controlled-start and negative-split plans. Each retains your requested finish time. A slower opening therefore requires faster later running. The methods page explains the curves; they are planning choices, not validated optimal strategies.

Use it at meaningful checkpoints

  • Compare a reliable course marker with the cumulative target.
  • Consider terrain, weather and effort before reacting to a difference.
  • Avoid a surge to recover time lost in a crowd or at a station.
  • Revise the goal if maintaining it would require an inappropriate effort.

At 30 km in 2:55, a four-hour finish requires about 5:20/km for the remaining 12.195 km. A band makes the difference visible; it does not make that later pace sustainable.

Prepare the reference

Check the selected distance, units, finish time and strategy. Use the generator’s wrist-band or checkpoint-sheet layout, print at actual size and check its 50 mm scale. Carry a readable version suitable for your race. These are preparation instructions, not a report of a physical field test.

Keep fuel reminders separate

The band contains pacing checkpoints. It does not include a gel schedule. Use the fueling calculator for separate reminders and the fueling guide to check totals and station access.

Generate your band

Open the custom generator or start from a goal page such as four-hour pace. Read GPS and course-line guidance if watch distance and course markers disagree.
