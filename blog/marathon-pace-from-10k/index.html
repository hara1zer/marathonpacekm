Marathon pace from 10K time

Your 10K is a strong fitness signal, but it is still much shorter than a marathon. That means a 10K-based marathon prediction can be useful, but only if you account for the extra fatigue, fueling, durability, and pacing discipline that the full marathon demands. This page gives you a practical calculator, a realistic prediction range, and the reality checks that stop a fast 10K from turning into an over-optimistic marathon goal.

What a 10K conversion calculates

A recent, representative 10K is a useful performance input when you do not have a comparable longer race. The conversion still assumes how your pace changes over a much larger distance.

Using T₂ = T₁ × (42.195 / 10)k, a 50:00 10K produces about 3:46:43 at k = 1.05 and 3:53:21 at k = 1.07. These are calculated scenarios. They do not establish the pace you can sustain today.

Why the model can miss

A prediction can miss because the model does not fit the runner, the input is unrepresentative, fitness changes, preparation differs or race conditions and execution intervene. It is not reasonable to assume that an error must be the runner’s fault.

Write down the input date and course context. A fast 10K months ago and a recent hilly half marathon answer different questions; averaging them does not remove that difference.

Read the labels as assumptions

The exponent choices 1.05, 1.06, 1.07 and 1.08 represent progressively more slowing with distance. There is no validated rule on this page that assigns them from weekly mileage, experience or long-run results.

Explore how sensitive the result is, then compare it with your whole training block and goal-selection questions. The slowest displayed scenario is not a guaranteed lower-risk result.

FAQ

Is a 10K enough to set a marathon goal?

It is one useful input. Preparation, recency, race conditions and model uncertainty still need review.

Which exponent is correct for me?

This calculator does not estimate your personal exponent. The choices show how a changed assumption affects the result.

Why might I miss even the conservative estimate?

The model may not fit, or the input, preparation, health, conditions and race execution may differ from its assumptions.
