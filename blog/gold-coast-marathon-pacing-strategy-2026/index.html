2026 archive and organiser sources

This page concerns the 5 July 2026 marathon in the Southport / Broadwater precinct. Use the organiser’s edition-labelled course material for the exact route and current event page when planning a later race.

A flat course does not remove the effort check

The useful advantage of a runnable course is fewer terrain disruptions. It does not establish that a shorter-race prediction or a fast first 10 km is sustainable over a marathon. Set the goal from current preparation and keep a second, slower plan available. The goal-time guide covers that review.

Plan changes of direction from the actual map

Coastal outbound and return sections can expose you to different wind directions. Older Gold Coast descriptions and map editions differ, so locate the current turns on the exact race map rather than memorising a turn point from a generic race description. Avoid assigning a kilometre or drink stop to a landmark unless it appears on that edition’s guide.

A four-hour opening example

At four-hour even pace, 10 km is 56:53. If crowding or headwind makes it 58:00, the remaining 32.195 km must take 3:02:00 to retain four hours, averaging 5:39.2/km. That is a modest arithmetic difference; it does not call for an immediate surge or establish that the pace will feel modest in a headwind.

Use comparable sections to inspect drift

Compare effort at measured markers on similar terrain, while recording whether wind exposure has changed. A slower return split can reflect a new headwind rather than a sudden loss of fitness. If effort is rising and the planned average no longer fits, revise the finish target. The effort and heart-rate guide explains that broader check.

Do not omit the final 195 metres

In the four-hour reference, 40 km is 3:47:31, leaving 12:29 for 2.195 km. Two kilometres alone would take about 11:23 at the same average. Use the whole measured distance when assessing the finish requirement.

A turnaround decision, not a fixed seconds penalty

Before each change of direction, notice current effort and the exposed wind. After turning, allow pace to change while effort settles. There is no universal seconds-per-kilometre penalty that fits every runner and gust. Do not weave or accelerate repeatedly to stay with a group whose pace requires more effort than your plan.

Wind pacing develops this decision.

Verify station access separately from timing

Use the organiser’s aid-station and refreshments guide to check the edition’s water, mix and product availability. Transfer your practiced elapsed reminders to that map. The worked schedules count grams from gels and verified drinks; a generic station label cannot fill a carbohydrate gap.

Save pacing and fuel as separate references

The pace-band generator prints elapsed checkpoints; it does not print gel reminders. Keep the fuel schedule separately. If you revise the expected finish, recheck both duration and cumulative pace checkpoints.

Make the edition check part of the plan

  • Save the map with the correct year visible.
  • Check your expected race-hour temperature and wind, not just a general winter description.
  • Verify product and station access against the elapsed reminders.
  • Use measured markers and total elapsed time for the goal check.

For a future edition, confirm start arrangements and cutoffs directly with the organiser.

Gold Coast archive FAQ

Does flat mean I should bank time early?

No. The route’s terrain does not establish your sustainable effort. The opening example calculates the remaining requirement without recommending a surge.

Can I reuse old turn points and stations?

Check them against the exact edition’s map and refreshments guide first. Generic descriptions can refer to a different route.
