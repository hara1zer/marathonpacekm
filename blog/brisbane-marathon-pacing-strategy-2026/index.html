The two-lap context belongs to 2026

2026 archive: the race took place on 7 June 2026. Qrun’s 2026 event listing describes the two-lap marathon and Story Bridge / riverfront context. The organiser page now promotes 2027. This archive does not establish a future edition’s lap format, route or station layout.

Compare the conditions across laps

A later lap occurs later in the morning, so temperature, sun and wind can differ even on the same road. Use the expected race-hour forecast when planning, and note conditions when comparing laps afterward. A cool first section is not evidence that the finish will require the same effort.

Use halfway, not a rounded 21.1 km assumption

A marathon half is 21.0975 km. Two rounded 21.1 km distances add to 42.2 km, not 42.195 km. Use the organiser’s measured timing location for a lap comparison rather than assuming every lap marker is exact halfway. The even four-hour halfway reference is 2:00:00.

Write a review before you see the second lap

At halfway, record elapsed time, perceived effort, fuel consumed and whether comparable course sections changed with wind or crowding. In a four-hour plan, arriving at 2:02:00 leaves 1:58:00 for the final half: 5:35.6/km. Do not call that simply “stay at the same pace”.

Compare effort before trying to reproduce the first lap

The same bridge or riverfront section can be a useful reference, but it now comes with more accumulated fatigue. If repeating the earlier pace requires a markedly higher effort, decide whether to revise the goal. Forcing the missing two minutes back through surges is a different plan from even effort.

Avoid overinterpreting GPS near course changes

Keep total elapsed time visible and check measured markers after the bridge or a crowded turn. One distorted instant-pace reading is not a reason to accelerate. GPS and tangents explains how watch distance and course distance can differ; hill pacing covers effort on climbs.

The second pass does not replace the elapsed schedule

On a repeated course, station landmarks recur, but elapsed fuel times do not reset at the start of the next lap. A practiced reminder at 2:15 remains 2:15 into the race. Verify products and access from the edition’s organiser guide, then compare with the fueling calculator and worked menus.

Keep cumulative elapsed checkpoints

Print one whole-race reference in the generator, rather than resetting the timer or reading a half-marathon band twice. The band prints pace checkpoints; fuel reminders remain in a separate schedule.
