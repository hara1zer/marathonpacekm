400 m pace chart and track split calculator

By Davin Pinto · Published 4 November 2026 · Updated 10 November 2026

At 5:00/km, one measured 400 m repetition takes 2:00. At 8:00/mile it takes about 1:59. Use the track split calculator to convert your prescribed pace, or work backwards from a recorded lap time.

The chart below covers 200, 400, 800, 1,000 and 1,600 m. It describes constant-speed arithmetic. It does not choose an interval-training intensity or predict an all-out 400 m race result.

How fast is a 400 m lap at my running pace?

Use minutes:seconds, such as 5:00/km or a 2:00 lap. Tenths of a second are accepted. Conversion assumes a measured 400 m distance.

Original arithmetic by Marathon Pace KM. 1 mile = 1.609344 km. Times are rounded once to whole seconds. A 1,600 m repetition is four measured 400 m laps; it is 9.344 m shorter than a mile.

How do recoveries change the full workout?

A repetition time is only part of the session. For a hypothetical 6 × 400 m at 5:00/km, each repetition is 2:00 and the running repetitions total 12:00. If there are five 90-second recoveries between repetitions, they add 7:30. The main set is therefore 19:30.

Add a 10-minute warm-up and 10-minute cool-down and that example takes 39:30. A sixth recovery after the final repetition would be an additional 1:30; do not silently include it in one version and omit it from another. Any distance covered while jogging recoveries also belongs in the recorded session distance.

These numbers explain accounting, rather than recommending six repetitions or a particular recovery. Choose the session and effort from your established programme. The tempo, threshold and marathon-pace guide explains why the purpose of the work matters before converting its pace.

For a longer repeated-distance example, the Yasso 800s worksheet counts ten repetitions and nine intervening recoveries. For marathon-specific formats, use the workout comparison. The conversion tool here does not turn a lap time into a validated marathon prediction.

Is every lap in every lane exactly 400 m?

A standard outdoor track's 400 m reference follows a defined measurement line in the innermost lane. An un-staggered full lap in an outer lane is longer. A marked staggered start can compensate for the different path length when the event is run in lanes. Follow the appropriate markings and venue rules rather than treating every line as the same start.

World Athletics' facilities and marking documents explain the measurement lines. Tracks and informal circuits can have different dimensions; confirm the venue before using a lap as an exact distance. A phone or watch GPS trace around tight bends is not a track survey.

For a recorded 1:50 over 400 m, pace is 110 ÷ 0.4 = 275 seconds/km: 4:35/km. The mile equivalent is approximately 7:22.6/mile. If the distance was not 400 m, that calculation answers the wrong question even when the stopwatch is accurate.

Find access and session-specific information in the Melbourne running tracks directory, including Sprint Athletics Centre and Hagenauer Reserve. Keep faster running clear of other track users according to the venue's instructions.
