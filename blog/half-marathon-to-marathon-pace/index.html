Use the half as evidence, with context

A half marathon is closer to the duration and distance of a marathon than a 5K or 10K, so a representative recent result is useful context. It still leaves a large additional demand untested.

My Run Melbourne comparison shows a stronger 2026 finish, including final full kilometres of 4:53 and 4:51. That was encouraging evidence about the half marathon. My subsequent Sydney result showed why it was not proof of a sub-four marathon.

Why the prediction can miss

The model can be wrong even when you execute the race well. Its endurance assumption may not fit you; the input may be old or unrepresentative; and marathon preparation, conditions, health and race decisions can differ.

A GPS activity with a recorded-distance problem is especially unsuitable for a precise race-time conversion. In my 2025 record, the note about distance loss is a reason to use the lap pattern descriptively rather than reconstruct a missing half-marathon result.

FAQ

Is the half always the best input?

No. Its longer duration is useful, but a representative recent race matters more than automatically preferring an old or compromised result.

Can I just double my half time?

Doubling preserves average pace over twice the distance. The power-law model allows slowing, but its chosen exponent may not match you.

Does a strong half-marathon finish prove marathon readiness?

No. It describes capacity late in that half marathon. The full distance adds demands that remain untested.

Which mode should I choose?
