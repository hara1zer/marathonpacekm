Marathon Pace From 5K Time

You can estimate marathon pace from a 5K, but it is one of the easiest ways to set an over-optimistic marathon goal. A 5K shows speed and VO₂-oriented fitness well. The marathon asks for something bigger: durability, fueling, pacing discipline, and the ability to keep the same effort economical for 42.2 km.

Calculator (Riegel-style model)

A widely used prediction approach uses a power-law relationship between time and distance, often referred to in running calculators as the Riegel model. It is useful, but the farther you project from the source race, the more careful you should be. Going from 5K to marathon is a very large jump, so conservative assumptions matter.

Why the endurance assumption matters

A 5K result records performance over 5 km; it does not directly measure VO₂max, threshold or marathon durability. Extending it to 42.195 km requires a large extrapolation. The model can be too optimistic or too pessimistic for an individual.

The formula is T₂ = T₁ × (42.195 / 5)k. At 25:00 for 5K, k = 1.06 gives about 3:59:47 and k = 1.08 gives about 4:10:14. Changing one assumption moves the estimate by more than ten minutes. Neither output is a ceiling on your potential.

Interpret the scenarios before choosing a goal

The menu labels describe relative positions within this calculator: a higher exponent allows more slowing as distance increases. They are not categories assigned by a validated assessment of your mileage, long runs or experience.

Review a representative longer race if available, the consistency of your training and the actual race conditions. A slower scenario can still be too optimistic. Use goal selection to turn the estimate into a considered target.

Choose representative inputs

A representative recent 10K or half marathon gives information over a longer duration. It can therefore be useful alongside a 5K, but an old, injured or unusually hilly race may be a poor comparison.

Combining results does not automatically improve accuracy. The combined calculator uses stated fixed weights; it is a planning heuristic, not a fitted individual prediction model.

Reality checks before you trust the number

Before locking in your marathon target from a 5K, run through these checks:

  1. Does the estimate agree with your 10K or half marathon?
    If not, the longer race is usually the safer anchor.
  2. Can you run marathon pace in training without it feeling like threshold?
    Use the marathon pace readiness test.
  3. Can you structure long runs around it?
    Use long run structure guidance.
  4. Do your early-race pacing habits support the target?
    Review how to know if you're overpacing and how to pace the first 10K.
  5. Can you fuel the race properly?
    Use the Marathon Fueling Calculator and fueling guide.
Practical recommendation If you only have a 5K, start conservative and aim for calm, even pacing. You are much more likely to run your best marathon by being slightly cautious early than by trusting the most aggressive 5K-derived number.

FAQ

Can a 5K predict my marathon exactly?

No. It is a starting point for a model with assumptions about slowing over distance.

Is the conservative result guaranteed to be achievable?

No. The label only means a slower model scenario, not a safety margin or confidence limit.

Should I add a half marathon result?

A representative recent longer race can add context. Check its date, distance, conditions and execution before comparing estimates.
