Treadmill pace chart: km/h, mph, min/km and min/mile

By Davin Pinto · Published 25 October 2026 · Research and worked examples

The treadmill says 10.0. Your training plan says 6:00/km. Those are the same speed—provided the treadmill is showing kilometres per hour. On a machine set to miles per hour, 10.0 is a very different run.

This treadmill pace chart puts km/h, mph, minutes per kilometre and minutes per mile together. Use the converter for an exact setting, or print the table for your next indoor session.

How to convert treadmill speed to running pace

A speed describes distance per hour. A pace describes time per kilometre or mile. Increasing speed makes the pace number smaller.

Pace in seconds/km = 3,600 ÷ km/h
Pace in seconds/mile = 3,600 ÷ mph
km/h = mph × 1.609344

At 12 km/h, divide 3,600 by 12: 300 seconds, or 5:00/km. At 6 mph, divide 3,600 by 6: 600 seconds, or 10:00/mile. That second setting is about 9.66 km/h and 6:13/km.

Be careful with decimal minutes. 5.5 minutes means 5:30, not 5:50. Enter paces in minutes:seconds in the converter. A prescribed 5:30/km is 330 seconds per kilometre, giving 3,600 ÷ 330 = 10.909… km/h. A machine with 0.1 km/h steps would offer 10.9 km/h, approximately 5:30/km.

Translate the whole workout, including recovery

Suppose your existing programme calls for 10 minutes easy, three 5-minute blocks at 5:30/km with 2-minute easy recoveries between blocks, then 10 minutes easy. If you choose 9 km/h for the easy running and 10.9 km/h for the faster blocks, the session is:

  1. 10 minutes at 9 km/h. Approximately 6:40/km.
  2. 5 minutes at 10.9 km/h, then 2 minutes at 9 km/h. Repeat that pair twice.
  3. 5 minutes at 10.9 km/h. There is no additional interval recovery after the last block.
  4. 10 minutes at 9 km/h to finish.

That is 39 minutes: 20 easy warm-up/cool-down minutes + 15 faster minutes + 4 recovery minutes. At constant stated speeds it covers 6.325 km. Real distance differs slightly while the belt accelerates and slows. The example explains a conversion; it does not choose a workout or pace for you.

Check what the session is intended to train before translating it. Tempo, threshold and marathon pace are not interchangeable labels. Use the monthly planner to organise an appropriate block and the race pace calculator for target-time arithmetic.

Does 1% incline make treadmill pace equal outdoor pace?

Incline changes the work required. It does not change the conversion: 10 km/h still corresponds to 6:00/km at either 0% or 1% grade.

The often-cited Jones and Doust study tested nine trained men in short bouts at specified speeds. A 1% grade gave a useful energetic comparison with outdoor running in that experiment. It did not establish an exact setting for every runner, treadmill, speed or session.

Choose incline for the purpose of your run and how you tolerate it. A hill workout and a flat easy run have different demands. Avoid treating an incline chart as a precise race-time prediction, particularly when indoor temperature, airflow and belt calibration differ. If you deliberately add climbing, the hill pacing guide explains why effort and speed can move in different directions.

Why does my watch show a different treadmill pace?

The console reports the machine's speed or distance estimate. An indoor watch usually estimates distance from movement or a connected sensor. Neither display is automatically a measurement of the belt's true speed under your load.

First check the units on both devices. Then compare a steady section rather than a few seconds after changing speed. Holding the handrails, changing arm movement, acceleration and different calibration settings can complicate the comparison. Follow your device's calibration instructions; a disagreement by itself does not identify which device is wrong.

For your training record, note the machine setting, incline, elapsed duration and effort. That gives you a more repeatable comparison than editing every run until two estimates match. The guide to training pace trends explains how to compare sessions without overreading one number.

Quick treadmill conversions

What treadmill speed is a 6-minute kilometre?

Exactly 10 km/h, or about 6.21 mph. At that constant speed, 30 minutes covers 5 km.

What pace is 8 mph?

7:30 per mile, or approximately 4:40/km. The equivalent speed is 12.874752 km/h.

What treadmill setting matches a four-hour marathon?

42.195 ÷ 4 = 10.54875 km/h, about 6.55 mph. This is the mathematical race average. It does not mean every training run should use that setting, or that holding it indoors establishes marathon readiness.
