Half-marathon taper

By Davin Pinto · Published 4 November 2026 · Updated 10 November 2026

Use the final days before a half marathon to reduce training demands within the plan you already follow. Start with what you normally run, choose the days that fit, and keep the race's 21.0975 km separate from the training budget.

Use the worksheet to plan your last six days. The 40 km baseline and 50% training budget are example inputs you can change.

What does a half-marathon taper change?

A taper reduces the training demand before competition. Distance, the amount of faster work, the number of sessions and their placement are separate decisions. Keeping a familiar short session does not mean keeping the full volume of hard repetitions. Your previous workload and recovery help determine what is a reduction.

A 2023 review by Wang and colleagues examined tapering across endurance sports and found performance benefits under several approaches. It does not validate this worksheet, establish a universal half-marathon calendar or imply that a recreational runner should add an overload block before tapering.

Decide the broader taper with your existing programme or coach, then use the worksheet to check the final distance allocation. A runner already struggling with illness, injury or persistent fatigue needs that issue addressed rather than a percentage calculation used as clearance.

How does the final week fit my usual running days?

Enter a representative completed weekly distance in your chosen unit. Choose the share you intend to run before the race. Give each of the six preceding days a relative share: zero leaves a day free; larger numbers allocate more of the same total. This distributes a budget, rather than choosing safe sessions.

Relative share on each day before the race

The default three-run distribution is one example. Keep or change the days to fit the routine you already follow. Changing km/miles reinterprets the entered numbers; it does not convert them.

Daily distances are allocated to tenths so that the displayed days add up to the displayed training budget. A zero is an unallocated running day; it is not a prescription for other exercise.

Why can race week total more than a normal training week?

With a 40 km baseline and a chosen 50% pre-race budget, training is 20 km. Distributing it in shares of 3:2:1 gives 10.0, 6.7 and 3.3 km after display rounding. Those runs add back to 20.0 km.

Adding the 21.0975 km event makes the total 41.0975 km, before any race-day warm-up or cool-down. That does not mean the training budget was 102.7% of baseline. It means the race was included in a different total. Keep the two comparisons labelled.

If you add a 1 km warm-up, enter it separately: 20 + 1 + 21.0975 = 42.0975 km. Warm-ups on the earlier training days belong inside those training distances. Avoid counting the same distance in two places.

What belongs in each remaining session?

A kilometre budget cannot decide the balance of easy running and faster work. Follow the session purpose in your established plan. Count the whole session, including warm-up, recoveries and cool-down, when fitting it into a day's allocation. A 5 km entry is not permission to add five fast kilometres after an uncounted warm-up.

Place runs on the days you normally use before considering an extra day. A runner accustomed to three days does not need to copy the frequency of a six-day schedule. Likewise, removing every familiar short run may change the routine more than intended. If an allocation looks awkward, change its share or the total with your plan rather than forcing a session to fill a cell.

Keep strength, cross-training, travel and standing time visible outside the mileage worksheet. A short running total can sit beside a demanding gym session or a long travel day. This tool does not convert those demands into equivalent kilometres.

Connect the last days to the decisions you need at the start

Use the half-marathon chart to check the target's arithmetic and the pacing strategy guide to decide how to begin. Print the pace band before the final evening so that sizing or printer settings are not a start-line problem.

Organise the familiar food and race-morning routine you have rehearsed. The carb-loading worksheet explains daily carbohydrate arithmetic and its scope; it does not mean every half-marathon runner needs the same loading strategy. A faster race estimate does not establish an individual nutrition requirement.

For a longer build, the monthly planner deals with a different time horizon. For the full marathon, use the existing two- and three-week marathon taper comparison. Neither should be copied unchanged into this final six-day worksheet.

Research, assumptions and limits

  • Wang and colleagues (2023): Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes. General endurance evidence, not a validation of this calendar or individual percentages.
  • Our calculation notes. Budget multiplication, relative shares, display allocation and race-distance accounting.

The examples and day weights are Marathon Pace KM illustrations. No personal successful taper, individual coaching review or universal half-marathon taper duration is claimed.
