About Marathon Pace KM

Pace charts, split calculators and printable bands for marathon runners.

Davin Pinto, site creator

I’m a recreational runner in Melbourne. I started running in late 2024, initially aiming to finish a half marathon. My recorded July 2026 half was 1:50:57. A month later, I went into Sydney Marathon aiming for sub-four and missed it.

I built Marathon Pace KM while trying to make sense of my own training and race plans. I wanted pace charts in kilometres, splits I could take to the start line, and calculators that showed their assumptions.

My Sydney review covers the training, pacing and fueling mistakes I want to learn from. The comparison of my two Run Melbourne halves includes the recorded laps and charts: I faded in 2025 and finished much better in 2026.

What I’m working on

I want to finish my next marathon strongly. That means training I can repeat, controlled workouts and a fueling plan I can follow when tired. I’ve written about my rebuild plans and experience with homemade and commercial gels in the Running Lab.

Using the site

Start with the pace chart, calculator or printable pace band. The guides cover training, pacing and fueling. Kilometres come first, with mile conversions where useful.

This is general running information and personal experience, not individual coaching or medical advice. Calculations and estimates are explained in the methods; the editorial policy covers sources and how I use software.

Corrections and suggestions

If a calculation looks wrong or a page is missing something useful, send me the details.
