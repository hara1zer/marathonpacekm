Norwegian Singles in marathon training: protect the long-run purpose

Count threshold work and marathon-specific work together. This page shows a concrete scheduling choice rather than treating threshold training as a complete marathon plan.

Use it for a defined part of the block

Controlled threshold work can be one component of a marathon plan. It does not supply the long-run practice, race-duration fueling or course decisions. Begin by identifying the purpose of the session you are adding and what other demand it replaces.

Write the quality budget explicitly

Suppose Tuesday contains 4 × 6 minutes of controlled work and Sunday contains three ten-minute marathon-effort blocks within a long run. That is 24 plus 30, or 54 planned work minutes, before counting the rest of the long run. These are different intensities, so adding the minutes is a workload inventory, not a physiological equivalence.

Add easy days before adding more quality

The three-to-six-day calendars keep the same one-session example while changing easy-running frequency. More days are not evidence that another threshold session belongs in the marathon block. Count your club session, hills and races too.

Assess how the long run finishes

Record later effort, fuel consumed and the next day’s response. In my 32 km rehearsal, early full laps were around 5:16–5:31/km and the last full laps eased to 6:28 and 6:47/km. The distance alone did not show a controlled finish.

Keep threshold and marathon labels honest

My Sydney build included work labelled marathon pace that was close to my recent half-marathon pace. The recorded workout examples illustrate why intended labels need to be compared with actual effort and preparation. A calculator output cannot establish a physiological threshold or marathon durability.

Find demands hidden in the week

  • Counting a fast long run as an easy day.
  • Adding threshold to an unchanged interval-and-race schedule.
  • Raising work minutes while the long run is also increasing.
  • Using a successful repetition session as proof of a finish-time goal.

When the long run is repeatedly compromised

If you keep cutting the long run short or struggle to resume normal easy training, review the whole workload before adding another session. The suitability checklist distinguishes a scheduling problem from signs that need a different response.

Marathon-block FAQ

Should I keep threshold work in every long-run-specific week?

Not automatically. The comparison here replaces Tuesday’s quality when Sunday becomes the priority. That is an illustrative tradeoff, not a universal schedule.

Can threshold pace predict my marathon pace?

It cannot establish marathon readiness by itself. Recent racing, repeatable training, long-run execution and conditions all belong in the goal review.
